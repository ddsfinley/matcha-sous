How is Sous different from an electric frother?

A frother spins a whisk head through the liquid on a shaft. Sous turns a disc magnetically from beneath a sealed stainless cup. The vortex mixes from the bottom up, at a calibrated speed, for a set time.

Do I have to give up my bamboo chasen?

Not at all. Many owners keep whisking by hand when the moment invites it. Matcha Sous reproduces the chasen’s fast, shallow agitation, so the bowl you love is ready even when the morning is short on time.

Does the mixer actually change how my matcha tastes?

It changes how consistently it tastes like itself. Three things decide a bowl: sifting, water temperature, and agitation. You control the first two at the counter; the third is the one that drifts with the morning. Matcha Sous holds the agitation identical every cycle, so a good tin tastes the way it should every time rather than only on your best attempts.

What matcha works best?

Any quality ceremonial or culinary matcha. Sous cannot fix a poor one, because it draws out what is already in the powder, so buy the best you reasonably can and you will taste all of it. Sift 1–2 teaspoons into the cup so the vortex can work fastest, and fill water to an etched 40, 80 or 120 ml guide.

What do the 18 speeds and 3 presets do?

The presets cover the common bowls: a gentle stir, an everyday bowl, and a rich latte base. The 18 manual speeds let you fine-tune agitation for volume, temperature and texture.

Can it make hot and iced matcha lattes?

Yes. Whisk matcha with a small amount of water first, letting the upper speeds build a rich concentrate, then pour it over hot milk or ice.

How loud is it?

It is a quiet hum, not a blender note. The whisk is driven contactlessly through the cup floor, so there is no gearbox or shaft noise.

How do I clean it?

Rinse the stainless cup and the whisk by hand and wipe the base with a damp cloth. Do not submerge the base. There are no seals, sockets or delicate parts to maintain.

How is it powered?
