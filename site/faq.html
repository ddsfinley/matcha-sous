Is Matcha Sous a milk frother?

No. A milk frother is primarily designed to introduce air. Matcha Sous was designed specifically around matcha preparation. The first stage brings the tea evenly through the water, while the second changes pace to reduce larger bubbles and refine the finish.

Why does Sous use two speeds?

Because the work of bringing matcha and water together is not exactly the same as the work of finishing the cup. The faster first stage handles incorporation. Once that is complete, Sous slows down to settle larger bubbles and refine the surface.

What is the second speed doing?

The finishing stage works down the larger bubbles created during the first part of the cycle and leaves behind a finer, more polished surface. The goal is not simply more aeration. It is a more controlled finish.

Will it create a mirror finish?

The second stage is designed to move the matcha toward the smooth, glossy surface many matcha drinkers look for. The exact result will still depend on the tea, dose, water, temperature and setting, but the finishing stage is there specifically to improve what happens at the surface after the matcha has been fully incorporated.

Do I need to sift?

We recommend it. Matcha compresses easily in the tin, and sifting gives the preparation a more even starting point. With Matcha Sous, water goes into the vessel first, then the matcha is sifted directly over it.

Does it replace a chasen?

No. A chasen remains an exceptional way to prepare matcha and can be part of an experience worth preserving. Sous simply offers another way to prepare matcha when you want greater repeatability, hands-free preparation or the pleasure of having that part handled for you.

Does Matcha Sous make matcha better?

It cannot improve the quality of the tea itself. What it can do is make a polished preparation easier to achieve and easier to repeat, especially when the finishing technique is something you have struggled to reproduce consistently by hand.

Can I adjust the settings?

Yes. Three presets cover everyday preparation, while eighteen manual speeds give you room to dial in a particular tea or recipe.

What are the etched markings for?

The stainless-steel vessel includes 40, 80 and 120 ml guides. Use less water for a concentrated latte base and more for matcha served straight.

How do I clean it?

Rinse the stainless-steel vessel and mixing component after use, then wipe the powered base with a damp cloth. Do not submerge the base.

Why is there a California Proposition 65 warning?

California requires a warning before a business exposes anyone in the state to any chemical on a list of roughly 900, at thresholds set well below the level at which harm has been observed. It is not a recall, a safety notice, or a finding about this product in particular, and the requirement applies to a great many ordinary household appliances. Because the warning attaches to the product as sold, it appears wherever the product is sold rather than only in California. The chemical we name is one associated with the flexible PVC used in cables and cords, not with the stainless-steel vessel or with anything that touches your matcha. You can read California’s own explanation at P65Warnings.ca.gov.
