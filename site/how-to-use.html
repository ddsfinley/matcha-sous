How to make perfect matcha.

Everything between the caddy and the cup: weighing and sifting, water and temperature, the mixing itself, then ice, milk and sweetener. A minute or less, start to sip.

Water first. Always.

Matcha dropped into a dry cup clumps on contact, and once it's clumped, no amount of mixing fully breaks it back up. Water in the cup first means the sifted powder disperses the moment it lands, instead of caking against dry steel.

That's why the order matters more than the exact amount: fill to whichever etched line suits your drink, then sift the matcha over it, never the other way around.

Starting recipes.

Every recipe starts the same way: water first, matcha sifted over it, then Sous. What changes is the finish.
