Good matcha begins with good tencha, but the preparation shapes what finally reaches the cup. Water temperature, ratio, the way the matcha is incorporated and the finish all matter, especially when you are working with a tea worth paying attention to.

If you already know exactly how you like yours, Sous is an easier way back to the same cup. If you have never quite had matcha at its best, this is where you find out what you have been missing.

A change of pace, by design.

It all happens within a single programmed cycle, with no need to stop, adjust or finish the matcha yourself.

01

The faster stage

When the cycle begins, Sous creates a faster vortex that draws the sifted matcha through the water, bringing the two together evenly without requiring a whisk in the cup.

02

The finishing stage

Once that work is done, the pace shifts. The slower stage settles the larger bubbles created during mixing and leaves behind a finer, polished surface. The kind of finish that can take practice to achieve consistently by hand.

Add water. Sift matcha. Select + start. Serve + enjoy.

04

Serve it straight, pour it over ice or finish it with milk. Rinse. Repeat.

Keep the chasen. Choose the tea.

There is real pleasure in making matcha by hand, and Sous was never meant to replace that. If you like the whisk, keep using it. Sous sits beside the chasen, not in place of it.

The tea stays your choice. What Sous adds is consistency: the same speed, the same duration, the same finish, first cup of the day or fifth. The whole cycle runs in about thirty seconds and asks nothing of you while it does.

Born from repetition.

We came to Matcha Sous from behind the bar, where repetition has a way of revealing what matters.

The tea could be weighed precisely, the water measured carefully and the recipe held constant, yet the final cup could still shift depending on the hand preparing it. That variability is easy to overlook when you make one matcha at a time. During service, it becomes much harder to ignore.

We were not interested in taking craft out of matcha. We were interested in giving one of the more difficult parts of that craft better support. That became Matcha Sous.
