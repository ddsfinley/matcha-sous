How to Make a Matcha Latte (Hot & Iced) at Home

The difference between a silky, vivid green cup and a dull, gritty one comes down to three things: your matcha, your water temperature, and how well you dissolve the powder before the milk goes in.

Matcha Sous Journal · Matcha lattes

A good matcha latte is one of the simplest things to make at home, and one of the easiest to get wrong. The difference between a silky, vivid green cup and a dull, gritty one usually comes down to three things: the quality of your matcha, your water temperature, and how well you dissolve the powder before adding milk. Once you understand how to make a matcha latte properly, you can recreate your favorite café drink for a fraction of the price, exactly to your taste.

This guide covers both the hot and iced versions, with measurements, temperatures, and the small habits that make a real difference.

What You’ll Need

  • Matcha: 1 to 2 teaspoons (2–4 grams). For lattes, a slightly stronger 2 g works well so the flavor carries through the milk.
  • Water: 60 ml (about 2 oz) at roughly 80°C / 175°F, just below boiling.
  • Milk: 180–240 ml (6–8 oz). Whole dairy milk froths beautifully; oat and soy are the most reliable plant options because they steam without splitting.
  • Sweetener (optional): honey, maple syrup, or a little sugar, to taste.
  • A way to whisk: a traditional bamboo whisk (chasen), a small electric frother, or a hands-free matcha mixer like Matcha Sous that handles the blending for you.

A quick note on why whisking matters: matcha doesn’t dissolve the way coffee or cocoa does. It’s a suspension of ultra-fine leaf particles, so without proper agitation it clumps and sinks. The goal is a smooth, slightly frothy base with no visible lumps.

How to Make a Hot Matcha Latte

Step 1. Sift the matcha. Push 2 g (about 1 heaping teaspoon) through a small fine-mesh sieve into your cup or bowl. Matcha cakes easily in the tin, and sifting is the single best way to prevent clumps.

Step 2. Add hot water. Pour in 60 ml of water at about 80°C / 175°F. If your kettle has no temperature setting, boil it and let it rest for 90 seconds. Water that’s too hot scorches the leaf and turns it bitter.

Step 3. Whisk into a smooth paste. Whisk briskly in a “W” or “M” motion for 15–20 seconds until the surface is uniform and lightly foamy. This concentrated base is where smoothness is won or lost. Matcha Sous, the hands-free matcha mixer, does this with a contactless magnetic whisk, bringing a bowl together in about 30 seconds, so you get a consistent base every time.

Step 4. Heat and froth the milk. Warm 180–240 ml of milk to roughly 60–65°C / 140–150°F and froth it. Don’t let it boil.

Step 5. Combine. Pour the milk slowly over the matcha base. Add sweetener if you like, stir gently, and serve.

How to Make an Iced Matcha Latte

The iced version follows the same logic, with one important rule: always dissolve matcha in a little warm water first. Matcha will not whisk smooth directly into cold milk.

Step 1. Make a matcha concentrate. Sift 2 g of matcha into a cup, add 60 ml of water at 80°C / 175°F, and whisk until smooth and frothy.

Step 2. Build the glass. Fill a tall glass with ice and pour in 180–240 ml of cold milk. Add sweetener now if using, since it dissolves better before the matcha hits the ice.

Step 3. Pour and finish. Pour the warm matcha concentrate over the iced milk. You’ll get that signature two-tone layered look. Stir before drinking.

For a thicker, café-style iced latte, shake the concentrate with a couple of ice cubes in a sealed jar for ten seconds before pouring. It chills the matcha and adds a fine foam.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using boiling water. It’s the number one cause of bitter matcha. Stay around 80°C / 175°F.
  • Skipping the sift. Lumps that form in the tin won’t whisk out fully.
  • Whisking matcha straight into milk. Milk proteins prevent the powder from breaking down. Always make a water-based concentrate first.
  • Using culinary-grade matcha and expecting sweetness. For a clean, naturally sweet latte, reach for a quality ceremonial-grade matcha.
  • Adding too much powder. More matcha means more bitterness, not more flavor. Start with 2 g and adjust.

Dialing In Your Ratio

Taste is personal. If your latte is too bitter, use slightly less matcha or a touch more milk. If it’s too faint, add half a teaspoon of matcha rather than reducing the milk. FAQ

Can I make a matcha latte without a whisk?

Yes. A small electric milk frother, a sealed jar you shake vigorously, or a hands-free matcha mixer will all break up the powder. The key is still to dissolve the matcha in warm water before adding milk.

What’s the best milk for a matcha latte?

Whole dairy milk gives the creamiest result and froths easily. Among plant milks, barista-style oat and soy are the most reliable because they steam smoothly without curdling against the matcha.

How much matcha should I use per latte?

Start with 2 grams (about one heaping teaspoon) per cup. That’s enough flavor to stand up to milk without turning bitter. Adjust up or down by half a teaspoon once you find your preference.
