Our story

The sous chef for matcha

We fell for the ritual long before we set out to refine it. Matcha Sous is our attempt to keep everything beautiful about the traditional bowl — and quietly remove everything that gets in its way.

A quieter morning
Where it began

A ritual worth keeping

Matcha Sous began in a small café where the morning rush met a bamboo whisk. The matcha was extraordinary — and impossibly inconsistent. One bowl sang; the next arrived grainy and grey. The difference was never the tea. It was the wrist.

We loved the calm of the ceremony, the sifting, the slow pour, the pause before the first sip. What we did not love was the learning curve, the lumps, and the way that good matcha was held hostage by technique. So we asked a simple question: what if the hardest part could simply take care of itself?

The answer was a sous chef. Not a replacement for the ritual — a quiet hand within it, doing the one job that asked the most and gave the least.

What guides us

Four things we refuse to compromise

Every decision, from the steel to the software, answers to these.

Craft

We honour a centuries-old practice with materials and engineering that earn their place beside it. Nothing decorative, nothing disposable.

Consistency

The first bowl and the four-hundredth should taste the same. Repeatable results are not a luxury — for hospitality, they are the whole point.

Calm

Tools should lower the temperature of a morning, not raise it. A whisper-quiet drive and unhurried design keep the ceremony peaceful.

Considered design

One cable, a small footprint, controls that wipe clean in a pass. We sweat the details you only notice when they are missing.

Made to last
The craft

Chosen for the long run

We could have moulded the pitcher from plastic and saved on every unit. Instead we drew it from food-grade stainless steel — the same alloy trusted in professional kitchens — with a seamless wall, etched measures and a spout shaped for a clean pour.

The whisk never touches your hand. A contactless magnetic drive spins it from beneath the base, so there is no gearbox to wear, no seal to leak and nothing fragile to replace. Backlit touch controls sit behind a single sheet of sealed glass.
